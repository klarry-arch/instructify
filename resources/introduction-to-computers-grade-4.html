03

Specific Learning Outcomes

By the end of the lesson, the learner should be able to:

Knowledge

  • (a) State the meaning of a computer as an electronic device that accepts, processes and stores data and produces information.
  • (b) Name at least four uses of computers in everyday life.
  • (c) Identify four common types of computers: desktop, laptop, tablet and smartphone.
  • (d) Identify the main parts of a desktop computer and state the function of each part.

Skills

  • (e) Switch on and shut down a desktop computer using the correct procedure.
  • (f) Open Microsoft Word and create a blank document.
  • (g) Type a heading and four simple sentences using capital letters, spaces and full stops correctly.
  • (h) Format a heading (bold, font size 16, centre alignment) and create a bulleted list.
  • (i) Save a document in the assigned folder using the correct filename.

Attitudes

  • (j) Follow computer laboratory safety rules.
  • (k) Share computers fairly and work with a partner cooperatively.
  • (l) Show care for shared digital devices.

4.6 Safety precautions

  1. Learners enter the lab only when the teacher is present.
  2. Bags, food and water bottles stay on the shelf at the door.
  3. Learners check that their hands are clean and dry before touching a computer.
  4. Only the teacher or lab technician switches on the main power at the socket.
  5. Learners never touch cables, plugs or sockets, and never open the system unit.
  6. Walkways stay clear of bags and cables.
  7. Learners report damaged keys, cables or screens immediately.
  8. Learners take a short eye break during the practical activity.
  9. Learners keep passwords private and use only their own account.
  10. Learners shut down using the Start menu, not by pulling the plug.

4.7 Assessment methods

  • Oral questions during each lesson stage
  • Observation with the teacher observation checklist (Section 10)
  • Practical task assessed using the analytical rubric (Section 9)
  • Matching activity (computer parts and their functions)
  • Written questions on the learner activity sheet (Section 12)
  • Product check: a saved "My School" document in the correct folder with the correct filename
  • Learner self-assessment: the completion checklist and reflection (Section 13)
  • Peer assessment: partners check each other's spelling, capital letters and full stops

Teacher’s Lesson Content

Use the explanations, examples, tables and scripts below to teach the lesson directly in class. Read the bolded definitions aloud and encourage learners to repeat key words together.

6.1 Meaning of a computer

Simple Grade 4 definition:

A computer is an electronic device that accepts data, processes data, stores data, and produces information.

Explain the four actions one by one using a familiar Kenyan Grade 4 school example: calculating a learner’s end-of-term marks.

StepWhat the computer doesSimple Grade 4 example (End-of-term marks)
1. Accepts dataTakes in raw facts (numbers, letters, or pictures) that we enter using a keyboard or mouse.The teacher types Amani’s test scores: English 42, Mathematics 45, and Science 40. These raw numbers are data.
2. Processes dataWorks on the data by adding, sorting, checking, or arranging it.The computer adds the three marks together: 42 + 45 + 40 = 127.
3. Stores dataKeeps the data and results safely in its memory so they can be used again later.The computer saves Amani’s marks in the Grade 4 report folder so the teacher does not lose them.
4. Produces informationShows or prints the final useful answer.The computer displays and prints Amani’s clean report card showing a total of 127 marks. That finished report card is information.

Quick oral check: Ask learners, "If I type the letters K-E-N-Y-A into a computer, is that accepting data or producing information?" (Answer: Accepting data.)

Computers make work faster, neater, and easier in many places around us:

PlacePractical Grade 4 examples of how computers are used
At schoolTeachers type exams, schemes of work and report cards; learners practise typing, reading and mathematics using educational games; headteachers keep attendance and fee records.
At homeLearners do homework and read e-books; families watch educational videos, listen to music, pay bills online and keep family photos safely.
In hospitalsDoctors and nurses store patient medical records, check laboratory test results, print prescriptions and track medicine stock in the pharmacy.
In banksBank tellers keep safe records of customers’ money, check account balances, process deposits and withdrawals, and run ATM cash machines.
In shops and supermarketsCashiers scan item barcodes at the till to calculate the total price quickly, print receipts and check which goods need to be restocked.
In transportBus companies, SGR train stations and airlines book passenger tickets, print boarding passes, display departure schedules and track vehicles on maps.
In communicationPeople send emails, write messages, make voice and video calls to relatives far away, and share news and school announcements quickly.

Key Pedagogical Note

Clearing up a common misconception (System Unit vs. CPU):

Do not tell learners that the whole box is called the "CPU." Explain clearly:

"This big box is called the system unit. Inside this box is a tiny chip called the CPU (Central Processing Unit). The CPU is the brain that does the thinking and processing, while the system unit is the protective box that holds the CPU, the memory, the storage drive and the power parts safely inside."

Categorising desktop computer parts

  • Input devices (put data into the computer): Keyboard, Mouse
  • Output devices (bring information out of the computer to us): Monitor, Speakers, Printer
  • Processing and storage components (work on and keep data): System unit (containing the CPU for processing and storage drives/memory for keeping data)

6.5 Computer safety, care, switching on and shutting down

Nine golden rules of computer safety and care

  1. Sit correctly when using a computer: Sit up straight with your back supported by the chair, feet flat on the floor, elbows relaxed, and eyes about one arm’s length from the screen. Good posture prevents back, neck and wrist pain.
  2. Keep food and drinks away from computers: Spilled water, juice or food crumbs can spoil keyboards and cause dangerous electric shocks. Always leave snacks and water bottles on the shelf at the lab door.
  3. Use clean and dry hands: Wash and dry your hands before entering the lab. Dust, oil and moisture damage keys and make shared equipment dirty for other learners.
  4. Handle the keyboard and mouse gently: Press keys lightly and move the mouse smoothly on the desk or mouse pad. Never bang keys, drop the mouse or pull its cable.
  5. Avoid touching cables and power sockets: Electricity is dangerous. Never plug in, unplug, pull or step on power cables and sockets. Call the teacher if anything comes loose.
  6. Keep the computer laboratory tidy: Walk calmly (never run), keep aisles clear of bags, and push your chair neatly under the table when leaving.
  7. Report damaged equipment to the teacher: If a key is missing, a wire is torn, a screen flickers or a computer refuses to start, tell the teacher immediately. Never try to fix it yourself.
  8. Protect personal information and passwords: Keep your login password secret. Do not share your home address, phone number or password with strangers, and never open another learner’s private work.
  9. Take short eye and movement breaks: Looking at a bright screen for too long tires your eyes. Every 10 to 15 minutes, look away at a distant object for 20 seconds, blink several times and gently stretch your fingers and shoulders.

Correct procedure for switching on a desktop computer

  1. Check that the computer, monitor, keyboard, mouse and cables are safely connected (look with your eyes — do not pull wires).
  2. Switch on the power source (wall socket / power strip) only when instructed by the teacher (in most primary labs, the teacher or technician turns on the main switches).
  3. Switch on the monitor by pressing the monitor’s power button once.
  4. Press the power button on the system unit gently once and release it.
  5. Wait patiently for the computer to start up and show the sign-in screen (do not keep pressing the button while it loads).
  6. Sign in using only the learner account and password provided by the teacher.

Correct procedure for shutting down a desktop computer

  1. Save and close all open documents and programs (such as Microsoft Word).
  2. Click the Start menu button (Windows icon at the bottom-left or bottom-centre of the screen).
  3. Click the Power icon.
  4. Click Shut down.
  5. Wait patiently until the screen goes dark and all lights and fan sounds on the system unit turn off completely.
  6. Switch off the monitor and power source only when instructed by the teacher.
Important Safety Warning

Never switch off a computer the wrong way!

Tell learners clearly: Never switch off a computer during normal use by pulling out the power cable, switching off the wall socket first, or pressing and holding the power button. Doing so can cause you to lose unsaved work, corrupt your saved files, and damage the computer’s operating system and hardware.

6.6 Introduction to Microsoft Word

What is Microsoft Word?

Microsoft Word is a word-processing application (computer program) used to create neat written documents.

With Microsoft Word, learners and teachers can:

  • Type documents (stories, letters, notes, compositions)
  • Edit text (fix spelling mistakes, add or delete words)
  • Format text (change font size, colour, bold, underline and alignment)
  • Insert pictures and shapes to make work attractive
  • Create lists using bullets or numbers
  • Save and print documents so they can be kept or shared on paper

Microsoft Word Practical Activity

Practical Task Title: "My School"

Time allowed: 20 minutes (guided in chunks, plus 5 minutes in Stage 7 for saving, closing and shutting down).

Step-by-step learner instructions

  1. Switch on the computer safely: Wait for the teacher to confirm power is on, press the monitor power button, then press the system unit power button once and wait.
  2. Sign in using only the learner username and password provided on your login card.
  3. Open Microsoft Word by clicking the blue "W" icon on the taskbar (or via the Start menu).
  4. Select Blank document so a clean white page opens.
  5. Type the heading: My School (remember capital M and capital S using the Shift key).
  6. Press the Enter key once to move the cursor to a new line.
  7. Type four simple sentences about your school. (You may write your own sentences or use the model text below.)
  8. Check punctuation as you type: Begin every sentence with a capital letter, leave one space between words, and end every sentence with a full stop (.).
  9. Fix typing mistakes by tapping the Backspace key gently and retyping the correct letter.
  10. Select the heading My School: Click just before the M, hold the left mouse button, and drag smoothly across My School until it is highlighted.
  11. Make the heading bold: On the Home tab, inside the Font group, click the B (Bold) button.
  12. Change the heading to font size 16: While My School is still highlighted, click the Font Size drop-down number box in the Font group and select 16.
  13. Centre-align the heading: While My School is still highlighted, go to the Paragraph group on the Home tab and click the Centre alignment button.
  14. Move the cursor below the four sentences: Click at the very end of your fourth sentence and press the Enter key twice to leave a clean blank line. (Check that Bold is turned off, Font Size is back to 11 or 12, and alignment is Left.)
  15. Type the subheading: Things Found at My School and press the Enter key once.
  16. Create a three-item bulleted list:
    • In the Paragraph group on the Home tab, click the Bullets icon (three dots with short lines).
    • Type the first school item and press Enter.
    • Type the second school item and press Enter.
    • Type the third school item.
  17. Check your document carefully: Read your heading, four sentences, subheading and three bullet items. Fix any spelling, capital letter, spacing or formatting mistakes.
  18. Save your document in the assigned folder:
    • Click File > Save As > Browse.
    • Open Documents > Grade 4 > [Your Stream].
    • In the File name box, type: My School - [Your Name] (for example, My School - Amani Otieno).
    • Click Save and check that your filename appears on the Title bar at the top.
  19. Close Microsoft Word correctly by clicking the X button at the top-right corner of the Word window.
  20. Shut down the computer safely: Click Start > Power > Shut down, wait until the computer turns off completely, then switch off the monitor only when the teacher instructs you.

Pair-work guidelines (for learners sharing a computer)

When two learners share one computer, display and enforce these four fairness rules:

  1. First half (Steps 1–13 — Opening, typing sentences, formatting heading):
    • Learner A operates the mouse (opening Word, clicking Blank document, selecting the heading, clicking Bold, Font Size 16 and Centre) while Learner B reads each step aloud from the activity sheet.
    • Learner B operates the keyboard (typing the heading and the first two sentences) while Learner A watches the screen and checks spelling, capital letters, spaces and full stops.
  2. Halfway role swap (Teacher announces "Swap roles!" after Chunk 2):
    • Learner A now operates the keyboard (typing the remaining sentences, the subheading, the bulleted list, and saving the filename) while Learner B checks spelling and punctuation.
    • Learner B now operates the mouse (clicking the Bullets button, navigating to File > Save As, closing Word and clicking Start > Power > Shut down) while Learner A reads the remaining steps.
  3. Both learners must demonstrate at least one practical skill directly observed by the teacher (e.g., Learner A demonstrates typing/bullets; Learner B demonstrates mouse selection/formatting or saving).
  4. Partner promise: "I help with my voice and pointing — I never grab the mouse or keyboard when it is my partner’s turn." When saving a shared document, pairs may save with both first names if working on one file (My School - Amani and Brian), or save quickly one after the other if time allows.

Follow-Up Lesson

Suggested Follow-Up Lesson Title

“Editing Text and Inserting a Picture in Microsoft Word.”

Connection to today’s lesson: Learners open their saved document (My School - Learner’s Name) from Documents > Grade 4 > [Stream] so they experience opening an existing file, improving it with editing and visual elements, and re-saving it cleanly.

Three Brief Learning Outcomes

By the end of the follow-up lesson, the learner should be able to:

  1. Open a previously saved Microsoft Word document from the assigned Grade 4 folder.
  2. Edit and format existing text by correcting spelling/punctuation, changing heading colour, underlining a subheading, and adding two new items to a bulleted list.
  3. Insert and resize one appropriate school-related picture neatly into a document and save the updated file correctly.

Follow-Up Practical Activity: "Upgrading My School Document" (25 minutes)

  1. Switch on and sign in safely following the computer lab routine.
  2. Open Microsoft Word, click File > Open > Browse, navigate to Documents > Grade 4 > [Stream], and open your saved file My School - [Your Name].
  3. Edit text: Read your four sentences carefully. Fix any spelling mistakes (look for red wavy underlines!), add one new sentence describing your favourite learning activity at school, and verify capital letters and full stops.
  4. Enhance formatting:
    • Select the heading My School and change its Font Colour to dark blue or dark green.
    • Select the subheading Things Found at My School and apply Underline (U) and Bold (B).
    • Click at the end of the third bulleted item, press Enter, and add two more items found at your school (five items in total).
  5. Insert and resize a picture:
    • Click below the last bullet item and press Enter twice so the bullet disappears.
    • Click Insert > Pictures > This Device, open the teacher’s offline School Pictures folder, choose one school-related image (e.g., library, playground, classroom, or books), and click Insert.
    • Gently drag one corner circle handle inward so the picture fits neatly on the same page without pushing text onto a second page.
  6. Save, close and shut down: Click the Save icon (Ctrl + S) to keep your improved work, close Microsoft Word using X, and shut down the computer safely via Start > Power > Shut down.
